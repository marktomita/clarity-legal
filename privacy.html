Clarity Finance Privacy Policy

Last updated October 24, 2026. This page: https://marktomita.github.io/clarity-legal/privacy.html. See also the Terms of Use.

This privacy policy applies to the Clarity Finance app (hereby referred to as "Application"), a Windows desktop application distributed via the Microsoft Store, that was created by Clarity Finance Software (hereby referred to as "Service Provider") as a Commercial service. This service is intended for use "AS IS".

Information Collection and Use

The Application is designed to run entirely on your local device. By default, the Application does not send any personal information, financial data or usage analytics to the Service Provider. It does save its automatic backups in your OneDrive folder when OneDrive is set up on your PC, and the OneDrive app then copies them to your OneDrive account; if you add the backup folder in Google Drive for desktop, Google Drive copies them to your Google account; see "Automatic backups" below.

All transaction data, account information, investment and property records, budgets, goals, category settings, language and currency preferences, and any files you attach to records, such as receipt photographs and PDFs, are stored on your device. The Windows app's automatic backups are also kept in your OneDrive folder when OneDrive is set up, and from there in your OneDrive account; if you add the backup folder in Google Drive for desktop, they are also kept in your Google account. They contain your transactions, accounts, investment and property records, budgets, goals, attached files and Clarity settings, including your display name, your recent searches, a record of your imports, the corrections autofill has learned, the entries you removed from the payee and budget-category suggestion lists, the record kept for the rating request (see "Rating Clarity" below) and your most recent AI analysis (the text your AI service returned). Each attached file is kept once, in a folder beside the backups, and shared by every backup that uses it. Backups written by this version and later leave out your AI connection details (service address, model, key and saved connections) and your AI chat; backups written by earlier versions also contain the service address, the model, your saved connections (their keys normally in encrypted form) and your AI chat, and they stay in the backup folder until 60 newer backups replace them or you delete them. Backups never contain anything the built-in browser keeps.

Investment, property and loan records are what you type in or import from a broker's file: trades, quantities, prices, commissions, holdings, options, futures, bonds, stock awards, property costs, improvements and values, the income and expenses you assign to a property, and a loan's terms. A broker file you import is read on your device and is not sent anywhere. The Application fetches no prices or quotes and makes no network request for them.

Data leaves your device in these circumstances and no others:

  • The AI Insights feature, which is inactive unless you supply your own AI service credentials and which you must then manually invoke. See "AI Features" below.
  • The optional assisted entry feature in the transaction form (Autofill transaction fields in Settings), which is off unless you turn it on and which also requires your own AI service credentials. See "AI Features" below.
  • Reading a receipt photo, which you start yourself and which also requires your own AI service credentials. The photo, reduced to at most 1,600 pixels on its longest side, and an instruction to read the payee, the total and the date go to your AI service; nothing else from your records is sent, and nothing is written until you confirm it.
  • Settings > AI > Test connection, which you press to check your AI settings. It sends one test word, with your AI service address, model and key, straight to your AI service; nothing from your records is included.
  • Automatic backups (Windows app). The Application checks shortly after it starts and then every half hour, and saves a new backup whenever your data has changed, and at least once a day; Settings can also save one at once. The newest 60 are kept, and each file you attach to records is kept once in a folder beside them. When OneDrive is set up, they are saved in your OneDrive folder and the OneDrive app copies each one to your OneDrive account under OneDrive's terms; without OneDrive they are saved in a Clarity folder in your user profile and stay on your device. If you add the backup folder in Google Drive for desktop, Google Drive copies each one to your Google account under Google's terms. What they contain is described above.
  • Files you save yourself go only where you put them: exports and saved PDFs; downloaded backups, which carry your attached files inside them; rolling backups, if you turn them on, which save one copy a day to a folder you choose, keep the newest 30 and keep each attached file once in a folder beside them; and, in the web version, a linked file on your PC that is rewritten with every change. If you save any of these to a folder that OneDrive or another cloud service syncs, such as a synced Documents folder, that service copies them. The same applies if you move the Windows app's data folder into a synced folder; the app warns you when your data folder is in one.
  • Speech. In the Windows app, the microphone in New Transaction, New Goal, New Budget and the AI chat box starts Windows' own speech recognizer, which runs on your PC, only while the microphone button is on; the Application receives only the recognized text and never records, transmits or retains audio. It is offered only when Windows has a speech recognizer for the language you use the app in; otherwise the microphone is hidden and a sentence you type is read on your device. In the web version, speech recognition is your browser's, not the Application's: when you use the microphone, your browser's speech service, which may be an online service run by your browser's maker, hears what you say and returns the text, under that browser's privacy policy. The Application receives only the text and never records, transmits or retains audio itself.
  • Camera and attached files. If you choose Take a photo when attaching a receipt, the Application turns on your camera, without sound, only while that window is open, and saves the one picture you capture as the receipt on your device. A picture or PDF you attach from a file is saved exactly as that file, including its name and any information stored inside it, such as where a photo was taken. One file can be attached to several transactions at once; it is stored once and kept until no transaction uses it. Attached files are never sent to the Service Provider, and never to any AI service except a receipt photo you choose to have read, as described above. They are included in your backups, so a cloud service that copies your backups copies them too; they are not included in exports such as CSV files and printed reports.
  • Web pages you open in the built-in browser (Windows app). The sites you open receive your requests directly from your device, as with any browser, under their own policies. See "The Built-in Browser" below.
  • Share buttons, only when you click them. See "Share Buttons" below.
  • Rating Clarity (Windows app). Rate Clarity in Settings, and a request to rate Clarity that the Windows app shows once, open the Microsoft Store's own rating and review page for Clarity. The request appears the first time a transaction is added after the Windows app has been opened on five different days and holds at least 20 of your own transactions (sample data does not count); whatever you answer, it does not appear again unless Settings > Reset everything deletes its record. That record is the dates of the last five days on which the Windows app was opened and whether it has asked; it stays on your PC and in your automatic backups and is never sent to the Service Provider. The Application sends nothing to the rating page. A rating or review you choose to post there goes to Microsoft, which shows it in the Microsoft Store and makes it available to the Service Provider, under Microsoft's terms and privacy policy.
  • Standard Microsoft Store telemetry collected by Microsoft as part of operating the Microsoft Store platform. This data is collected by Microsoft, not by the Service Provider, and is governed by Microsoft's privacy policy.

The Application has no analytics and sends no usage information: it does not track the pages you visit within it, does not log time spent, does not send your operating system details, and does not gather information about the location of your device. Your IP address reaches the Service Provider's systems only when you open Clarity's web app, or when a Windows app version or Clarity's web app before 1.0.658 sends an AI request through the relay described under "AI Features", and it is kept only as described there. The Windows app keeps a small diagnostic log on your PC (the app and Windows versions, the window's size and position, the folders it uses, and events such as backups and errors); it never records the pages you open in the built-in browser, stays on your PC and is never sent.

The Built-in Browser

Web pages you open in the Windows app's built-in browser keep their own cookies, site data and cache on this device, in a store of their own, apart from Clarity's data. Clarity never reads them, and never sends, syncs, exports or backs them up. No list of the pages you visit is kept in Clarity's logs, backups, exports or AI requests; to reopen your tabs, Clarity keeps the addresses of the tabs that are open, on this device only. Settings > Clear Browser Data removes the cookies, site data and cache, the open tabs and the choices you made for each site. The sites you open receive your requests directly and collect data under their own policies. If you choose Your Browser for web links, no page opens in the built-in browser, and Clarity offers to clear what it kept.

Other People's Details

The Application lets you record details about other people, such as payees, customers and vendors, and you are responsible for having the right to keep them. These details stay on your device and in your backups. They leave your device only in these cases: when autofill is on and you have set up an AI connection, the payee name you enter on a new transaction goes to your AI service; anything you type into the AI chat goes to your AI service when you press Send; when you use AI Insights, your Clarity data, including the payees, notes and other details you record about other people, goes to your AI service; a receipt photo you choose to have read goes to your AI service with whatever it shows; and files you export, print or save, and backups that a cloud service copies, carry them wherever you put them.

App Lock and Your Recovery Code

If you set an app lock, the Application keeps neither your passcode nor your recovery code: it keeps a check derived from the passcode and a lock key stored only in wrapped form, which your passcode, your recovery code or your device's own unlock opens. Your device's unlock (Windows Hello in the Windows app, or a passkey in Clarity's web app) is checked by your device, and the Application receives only its result. If you turn on encryption, your records are encrypted on this device with a key derived from that lock key, and a backup carries the wrapped key so that it opens on another device with your passcode or recovery code. None of this leaves your device except inside your own backups, and the Service Provider never receives your passcode, your recovery code or your keys. If you lose your passcode and your recovery code and cannot use your device's unlock, no one, including the Service Provider, can recover your encrypted data.

AI Features

The Application includes optional features that use AI: AI Insights, which provides AI-generated financial analysis, summaries, and answers to questions about your data; reading a receipt photo, which fills the payee, the total and the date of a new transaction; and assisted entry (Autofill transaction fields in Settings), which can fill fields on a new transaction. Filling in a transaction, goal or budget from a sentence you speak or type is done on your device and uses no AI service.

These features require you to supply your own AI service credentials. You choose the AI provider, the API endpoint address, and the model, and you enter your own API key in the Application's settings. The Application ships with no AI provider configured and no credentials of any kind. If you do not configure these settings, the AI features are unavailable and no data is transmitted through this channel.

Assisted entry is additionally off by default. Even with credentials configured, no request is made from the transaction form until you explicitly turn it on. Filling from your own history happens entirely on your device and involves no network request at any time.

Your API key is stored locally on your device in encrypted form whenever your device supports it; if it cannot be encrypted, it is stored as plain text rather than not at all. The AI service address and model are stored as plain text. The key is masked in the interface after entry, can be cleared by you at any time, and is not written in readable form to backup or export files created by the Application.

When you have configured credentials and use AI Insights (an analysis or a chat message), the Application sends your Clarity data from your device to the AI service you chose, with your API key in the request header your AI service requires: every transaction you have entered, with all of its details (payee, amount, date, type, category and subcategory, account, notes, tags, split parts, check number, recurrence, transactions scheduled for later dates, and the names of attached files, but not the files themselves); your accounts and the balance the Application computes for each; your categories, subcategories, budgets, savings goals, payee rules, transaction templates, saved filters, alerts, business records and the other records you have entered; and the totals the Application computes from them: income, expenses and transfers by year, quarter and month, and by payee, category, subcategory, account and tag in each year, with transactions dated after today totalled apart. It also carries a summary: income, expenses and net for the current month, for each of the last three months, for each month of the year to date, and for each month of the full calendar year including scheduled transactions that have not happened yet; each budget's category name with the amount spent, the limit and the percentage used; each savings goal's name with the amount saved, the target and the percentage reached, where a goal that follows an account counts that account's value, or the transfers into it less those out of it, as its amount saved; for budgeted categories and categories with spending this month, the name each is shown under in your language where that differs from its stored name; your currency code and symbol; and the language you use the app in. A run of eight or more digits anywhere in your records, such as a full account, card or routing number, is sent as its last four digits only, and your API key and other AI settings are never part of the data. Your display name and the contents of attached files are not sent. When your records do not all fit in one request, all the totals are still sent, the transactions your question names come first and then the most recent, the request says how many transactions it lists out of how many, and your AI service can ask for others: the Application answers such a request from the records on your device with the matching transactions and their totals, at most three times for each question or analysis. A request may be sent once more automatically, with the same content, at most once for each of these reasons: an analysis whose reply cannot be read; a refusal by your AI service of one of the request's settings, after which it is sent without the temperature setting and with the reply-length limit under the name or at the size the refusal gives; an empty reply from a model that used its whole allowance, after which it is sent with more room, up to 32,768 output tokens; and an AI service address given without a path that is not found at /v1/chat/completions, after which it is tried at /chat/completions.

In the AI chat, your Clarity data and the summary are sent again with every message, together with the conversation so far (up to the last 40 messages, including the AI's earlier replies). An analysis or a chat message you start keeps running if you open another part of the Application, another window or tab, or minimize the Windows app, and its answer is shown when you return; it is not sent again.

When autofill is on and you have set up an AI connection, each time you finish entering a payee on a new transaction the Application sends that payee name, and the names of the fields your own history could not fill (from amount, type, account, category, subcategory and note), to the AI service you chose. Amounts, dates, account names, balances and the rest of your records are not sent. Autofill has one switch: with an AI connection set up, turning it on also turns on these requests.

When you speak or type a sentence to fill in a transaction, goal or budget, the sentence is read on your device and is not sent anywhere; a question you speak into the AI chat box is sent to your AI service only when you press Send. Nothing is written to your records until you review the filled-in fields and confirm them with Save.

From version 1.0.658, these requests go straight from your device to the AI service you chose: in the Windows app from the Application itself, and in Clarity's web app from your browser. They do not pass through any server operated by the Service Provider. In the Windows app the address can also be on your own PC or local network (such as localhost); Clarity's web app accepts only https addresses on the public internet. Because the requests go straight from your device, your AI service sees your device's network address, as with any request you make to it.

Your API key travels in the request header your AI service requires: as a bearer token in the Authorization header, or, for the few cloud hosts that require it, in an api-key header.

The Service Provider does not receive, store, log, or retain the contents of these requests, the responses, or your API key. The Application reads an error reply from your AI service only to choose the message it shows you (for example, that the key, the model name or the address is wrong, or that your account is out of credit); that reply is never passed on, stored or logged.

Windows app versions and Clarity's web app before 1.0.658 send these requests through a proxy operated by the Service Provider, which the app calls Clarity's relay, until it stops answering on December 22, 2026. The relay keeps no copy of the requests, the responses or your API key, and reads an error reply only to choose the message the Application shows you. To enforce a limit of 20 requests per hour, it holds your IP address and the times of your recent requests in its working memory, never on disk or in its own logs; times older than an hour are dropped at your next request, and the whole record is lost whenever the relay restarts. Its hosting platform may keep routine records of each request, such as the time, the path, the response status and your IP address, under the platform's own privacy policy. No part of your financial data, your prompt, the response or your key appears in either. Your AI service sees the relay's network address, not yours, for requests sent this way.

Files you attach to records, such as receipt photographs and PDFs, are stored on your device and in your backups, and are not transmitted to the Service Provider or to any AI provider, except a receipt photo you choose to have read, which goes to your AI service as described above.

Once your data reaches the AI provider you selected, that provider's own privacy policy and terms govern how your data is handled, including whether it is retained or used for model training. The Service Provider has no relationship with, and no control over, the provider you choose. You are responsible for reviewing that provider's privacy practices before configuring it.

No data is sent to any AI provider unless you have configured credentials and then press Test connection, run an AI Insights analysis, send a chat message, have a receipt photo read, or turn autofill on.

Opt-Out Rights

Because your data is stored on your device and, when OneDrive or Google Drive copies your backups, in your OneDrive or Google account, you can stop all data retention at any time by uninstalling the Application and deleting that data. Uninstalling removes the Application but deliberately leaves your data folder and your automatic backups, so that an uninstall cannot lose your ledger. Settings > Data location shows both folders; delete them, and the backups in your OneDrive or Google account if either copies them, to remove all of your data.

You may also choose to never configure AI credentials, to leave assisted entry off, to never use dictation, or to clear previously entered credentials from the Application's settings, in which case no data leaves your device through the Application other than the automatic backups that OneDrive copies when OneDrive is set up or Google Drive copies once you add their folder, files you yourself save to a folder that a cloud service syncs, and the web pages, share buttons and map links you choose to open.

You can turn off the Application's access to your microphone or camera at any time in Windows Settings > Privacy & security > Microphone or Camera, or, in the web version, in your browser's site settings.

Settings > Autofill transaction fields > Forget what autofill learned deletes, after asking, the corrections autofill has learned from your edits on this device, and does not change your transactions. Copies already saved in automatic backups stay in those files until they are replaced or you delete them.

Data Retention Policy

The Service Provider does not retain user data on its servers. All user data, including any attached files and any AI credentials you enter, is stored on your device until you delete it, and what you delete stays in Recently Deleted for 30 days unless you clear it there; it is kept outside the Application's own folder, so it remains after an uninstall. Automatic backups are also kept in your OneDrive account when OneDrive is set up, and in your Google account if you add the backup folder in Google Drive for desktop.

Besides your records, the Application keeps on your device: your settings and layout choices; the display name you enter for the greeting, if any; your recent searches; a record of each import (file name, time and counts); the corrections autofill learns when you change a value it filled in; the entries you removed from the payee and budget-category suggestion lists; in the Windows app, the record kept for the rating request, described under "Rating Clarity" above; your most recent AI analysis and up to the last 40 AI chat messages, until you clear them; the items you delete, with their attached files, in Recently Deleted for 30 days, where you can restore or clear them, and which backups do not include; small diagnostic records, such as content the app's security policy blocked; a recent copy of your records used to recover from a damaged save; and, in the Windows app, what the built-in browser keeps, described under "The Built-in Browser" above. Settings > Reset everything deletes all of this from the device, but not backup files.

Backup files created by the Application contain your transactions, accounts, investment and property records, budgets, goals, settings and attached files, but not your AI settings, key or chat. A backup you download carries its attached files inside it; automatic and rolling backups keep each attached file once in a folder beside them, and a backup copied away from that folder restores its attached files only while the Application can still find them, in a folder beside the copy or in this PC's backup or rolling backup folder, and otherwise restores everything except them. A backup saved with Back Up Now in Settings also carries its attached files inside it. Export files, such as CSV files and printed reports, do not contain attached files.

If you have any questions about data the Application stores locally, you may contact the Service Provider at marktomita@gmail.com.

Security

The Service Provider is concerned about safeguarding the confidentiality of your information. Because user data and any AI credentials you enter are stored locally on your device, the security of that data depends primarily on the security of your device and operating system, and, for automatic backups that OneDrive or Google Drive copies, on the security of your Microsoft or Google account. Your API key is normally held in encrypted form, but that encryption protects the stored value only and is not a substitute for device security. The Service Provider recommends following standard device security practices including operating system updates, disk encryption, and strong account credentials. If you believe your device has been compromised, you should revoke your API key directly with your AI service provider.

For the limited data transmitted during AI requests, the Application uses HTTPS encryption in transit between your device and the AI endpoint you configured; a request the Windows app sends to an AI server on your own PC or local network uses the scheme that address names, which may be unencrypted http, and does not leave that network.
