Speech, Assisted Entry and Filled-In Entries

The Application can fill in a new transaction, goal or budget from one sentence you speak or type, and can fill fields on a new transaction from patterns in your own existing records; both happen on your device, and nothing is saved until you confirm it. With your own AI service set up, it can also read the payee, the total and the date from a receipt photo you choose; that photo goes to your AI service, and nothing is saved until you confirm it.

Filling fields from your own history is part of assisted entry and is off until you turn on Autofill transaction fields in Settings; if you have set up an AI connection, turning it on also lets the Application ask your AI service about the fields your history cannot fill, with no separate switch.

In the Windows app, a spoken sentence is recognized by Windows' own speech recognition on your PC and the Application receives only the recognized text; where speech recognition is not available, the microphone is hidden and you type the sentence instead. The Application never captures, transmits or retains audio. In the web version, speech recognition is your browser's: your browser's speech service, which may be an online service run by your browser's maker, hears what you say under its own terms and privacy policy, and the Application receives only the text.

Investments and Property

The Application lets you record investments, property and loans by hand, or import a broker's trade or activity file, which is read on your device and saves only the rows you confirm: trades, quantities, prices, commissions, holdings, options, futures, short sales, money borrowed on margin, bonds, CDs and Treasury bills, employer stock awards, property costs, improvements and values, the income and expenses you assign to a property, and a loan's rate and term. It fetches no prices or quotes, so values reflect the prices you enter and may be out of date. Sales are matched to your oldest purchases first unless you choose the lots, which may differ from the method your broker or tax authority uses. Options and bonds are valued at the last price you enter, and at their cost until you enter one; futures and employer stock awards are valued by the simple rules the Application shows beside them, such as counting unvested shares as nothing and moving each futures settlement into cash. None of these are market valuations. A loan payment is split into interest and principal from the rate and term you enter, and the split may differ from your lender's. Gains, losses, days held and yearly totals are calculated from your entries for information only; they are not tax figures, apply no tax rule, and do not replace the statements your broker or a qualified professional provides.
